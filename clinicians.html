For clinicians

cCMV is a timing problem before it is a diagnostic one

The assay question is straightforward. The operational question — getting a valid specimen collected and resulted before day 21, in a baby who looks entirely well — is where programs succeed or fail.

Diagnostic pathway

Specimen, method and window

QuestionCurrent guidance
Preferred specimenSaliva by PCR, with confirmatory testing on urine.
WindowWithin 2–3 weeks of birth. After 21 days, congenital infection cannot be distinguished from postnatally acquired infection.
SerologyAntibody testing cannot diagnose congenital CMV. Maternal IgG crosses the placenta; infant IgM is insufficiently sensitive.
Saliva collectionMany protocols advise collecting at least an hour after a breast milk feed, since CMV in breast milk can produce a false positive on saliva. Confirmation on urine resolves this.
Missed windowRetrospective PCR on a stored newborn dried blood spot may be attempted, but sensitivity is materially lower — a negative DBS does not exclude cCMV.

Presentation

What you will and will not see

Symptomatic at birth — about 10%

Petechiae, jaundice, microcephaly, intrauterine growth restriction, hepatosplenomegaly, seizures, retinitis. Thrombocytopenia and elevated transaminases are common laboratory findings.

Roughly 40–60% of this group will have long-term sequelae — sensorineural hearing loss, developmental delay, vision impairment, microcephaly, motor deficits or seizure disorder.

Asymptomatic at birth — about 90%

Clinically indistinguishable from an uninfected newborn. Most remain well.

A subset develop delayed-onset sensorineural hearing loss that the newborn hearing screen cannot detect, because hearing is normal at the time of screening. Across both groups, about 1 in 5 infected infants has a birth defect or long-term health problem.

The failure mode to design against

A well-appearing newborn passes the hearing screen, is discharged on day two, and presents at eighteen months with hearing loss. At that point cCMV is unprovable, treatment is no longer on the table, and the family has no explanation. Every screening decision you make sits upstream of that scenario.

Screening strategy

Universal versus hearing-targeted

Hearing-targeted screening

CMV testing triggered by a failed newborn hearing screen. Thirteen states currently require some version of this. Lower volume, easier to operationalise, and it captures the infants most likely to benefit from antiviral consideration.

Its limit: it misses infants with normal hearing at birth who go on to develop delayed-onset loss — a substantial share of cCMV-attributable hearing loss.

Universal screening

Every newborn tested regardless of hearing result. Minnesota implemented this on February 6, 2023 under the Vivian Act, the first state to do so; Connecticut’s requirement took effect July 1, 2025.

Its demand: a workflow that can collect and result a specimen on every baby inside the window — which is a throughput and turnaround problem, not a scientific one.

After a positive result

Management and follow-up

Evaluation

Confirm the diagnosis, then characterise it: physical examination with attention to growth and head circumference, complete blood count and hepatic panel, ophthalmologic examination, diagnostic audiologic assessment, and neuroimaging where indicated. The symptomatic versus asymptomatic distinction drives everything downstream.

Antiviral therapy

Ganciclovir or valganciclovir may improve hearing and developmental outcomes in newborns with signs of congenital CMV disease. Evidence is more limited for infants with isolated sensorineural hearing loss and for asymptomatic infants. Treatment carries meaningful toxicity, notably neutropenia, and requires monitoring — the decision belongs with pediatric infectious disease input.

Longitudinal follow-up

Regardless of treatment status, infants with confirmed cCMV need scheduled audiologic surveillance through early childhood, ophthalmologic follow-up, and developmental monitoring. Delayed-onset hearing loss is the reason a single normal result at birth does not close the case.
